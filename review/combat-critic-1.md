# Independent combat review — critic 1

**Verdict: functional combat passes this bounded inspection; reference-level presentation and responsive combat readability do not.** The wuxia scenery is richly detailed, the characters have recognizable silhouettes and real pose changes, and the basic tactical choices have visible costs and consequences. I would not call this comparable to the reference games' overall quality on the evidence inspected. The biggest remaining gap is the battle layout: in phone landscape, the player must scroll between the health/turn information and the action controls, losing the context needed to make the decision.

Reviewed the actual running game at `http://127.0.0.1:3017` on 2026-09-28 through Chromium/Playwright. All captures were completed before authorizing the builder to resume battle changes. This report describes those captures, not subsequent revisions. No production source, DESIGN.md, progress.json, builder reports, or old reviewer verdicts were read. The mandated shared.md had historical builder context; that exposure is disclosed. Only `tests/browser/game.spec.ts` was read for navigation/save mechanics. No production code was modified.

**Single highest-priority acceptance criterion:** at 844×390, immediately after battle entry and after either orientation change, both fighters, current actor, both HP values, player MP, and Attack/Guard/Recover costs and controls must be visible together without scrolling or zooming. Each principal control should have a target at least 44 px tall. Repeat a Guard→strike exchange and open the outcome; retain the same simultaneous visibility throughout. A compact landscape HUD with the stage and controls beside one another is an appropriate direction. Passing horizontal-overflow checks alone is insufficient.

The failure is reproducible: start a normal battle at 390×844, attack once, resize to 844×390. The Attack button begins at **y=648**, outside the 390-pixel viewport. The initial view contains HP panels and the upper scenery, with the fighters below the fold. Scroll the action into view and the HP/turn display disappears. The document itself reports no overflow because scrolling occurs inside the battle presentation. See [landscape initial view](combat-critic-1/mobile-landscape-top.png), [landscape controls after scrolling](combat-critic-1/mobile-landscape-controls.png), and [recorded bounds](combat-critic-1/mobile-observations.json).

**Visual judgment against the supplied references.** Before reading reference metadata, I compared my [actual desktop capture](combat-critic-1/A-battle-desktop.png) with the unlabeled [reference battle image](baseline/ref-2338140-1.jpg). My first judgment is preserved in [first-visual-judgment.md](combat-critic-1/first-visual-judgment.md). This is title-unlabeled comparison, not true blinding: I knew which screenshot I had captured, knew the requested titles, and could recognize the reference style/copyright. I did not play either reference game and make no reference-gameplay comparison.

The Dokapon reference makes the actor, matchup, HP, and turn immediately prominent through scale, contrast and composition. The current scene makes beautiful scenery prominent and asks the player to read small, similarly weighted UI text for the meaningful state. The green/gold palette has a coherent wuxia mood, but it needs a much stronger combat hierarchy. The reference's visual clarity is a concrete benchmark; copying its bright palette is unnecessary.

Against the local Hero's Adventure images [village](baseline/ref-1948980-0.jpg) and [bamboo environment](baseline/ref-1948980-2.jpg), the current art conveys detail and setting convincingly, but the reference uses clearer pixel clusters and quieter material areas. The current courtyard/background stack is densely textured almost everywhere. The fighters are readable at desktop size, yet lose priority to architecture and foliage, especially on phone. Detailed imagery alone does not establish the requested overall art quality.

A second presentation issue is contact: [wind-up](combat-critic-1/motion-019.jpg), [extended strike / impact](combat-critic-1/motion-025.jpg), and [recovery](combat-critic-1/motion-032.jpg) show actual pose changes, but the fist ends near x720 while the target begins around x825. The target flashes and recoils despite that gap. For a straight physical punch, bring the fist and target silhouette together at impact; if it is ranged qi, visibly connect the distance with an appropriate effect. This is more consequential to perceived combat force than adding further background detail.

The tactical basics are present: Guard visibly trades MP and a turn for reduced damage and a boosted strike; Recover trades a turn and a defensive penalty for MP. They are not decorative buttons. However, this starter matchup is almost a two-hit race, and the recorded exchange contains roughly four seconds of passive gauge filling before another decision. That observation was made under capture load, not a performance benchmark. The bounded runs establish working choices, not deep balance or sustained strategic enjoyment.
